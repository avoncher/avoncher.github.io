Put Your Phone Away!

Next time you are commuting or waiting in line, I want you to put your phone in your pocket and just be there. You can find many videos on YouTube which highlight the benefits of being bored. Many of my greatest ideas came to me whilst I was just thinking about things. Think about your shower thoughts but even more powerful!

I would even go a step further and say to avoid listening to music when you can too. Think about how you would feel about your favourite song if you kept listening to it on repeat, a similar thing will happen if you generally listen to music non stop. Experiment with not listening to any music for a while and then come back to it and you will feel a much stronger connection with all of your favourite songs.

Recently I went to a concert and I didn't take any videos of the concert. One thing I noticed is that it was easier to enjoy the really great parts because I wasn't worried about capturing the "best" moments and worrying if maybe this moment was even better than the previous and therefore needed to be videod.

Though in general I think it's just sad to look around and seeing everyone with their heads down and swiping, especially because when I look at their screen they're never doing anything urgently, usually it's just scrolling of some form. Embrace the boredom! It won't stick around for long and your mind will be wandering in no time.

Pen and Paper Notes

Either my smartest or my dumbest idea!

I'd really like to try next semester is trying to do as much of my work with a physical note book as I can. There are some benefits of this (in theory). It should be far harder to get distracted when you don't have the whole internet at your fingertips! There are of course the studies that show memory is better when handwriting instead of typing. It becomes harder to use language models in studying or doing work, and I believe there are studies showing that end result learning is better when you do not use these models.

Though I need to weigh how strict I will be with this. For example will I watch lectures on my laptop at times and then use a notebook, what about textbooks? And what about when I need to look something up? Will I let myself use the AI when looking stuff up? I don't know!

I think the biggest change will be being physically present in the lecture theaters taking physical notes without a device to conveniently distract me. I'll figure out the finer details when the time comes, but I think with things like this its important to not get lost in the purity or the aesthetics of the idea, but rather simply acknowledge the idea is just a possible path to get to a desired end point.

Almost Getting Hit By Cars

One of the most frustrating things of all time is when an unfocused driver of a car almost maims you with their car. It is particularly frustrating because almost every time it happens to me, the driver of the car gets angry that I had the the gall to be crossing the road at that time when they wanted to turn, despite people walking having the right of way, and despite me being in the road already. This anger is usually followed by angry honks, and then ear deafening revs of their car engine as they speed off like nothing happened.

The most frustrating part of it all, is just how powerless you are. You were forced to jump out of the way like a bird on the road. You almost got seriously harmed, possibly with broken bones and long lasting damage, your mood is just ruined for the rest of the day, and the car is the one who gets to speed off like nothing happened, and guess what? They're right: nothing ever fucking happens to them driving their car. We live in a world built for cars and the people who have the privilege of driving in one.

The only path forward is to build safer cities for people walking. We'll see if it ever happens...

Procrastination

Would you believe I procrastinated on writing this! And would you believe that I am writing this right now to procrastinate from doing an assignment!

As an expert procrastinator I can conclude the cause of procrastination is avoiding some negative feeling by distracting yourself. Maybe among the astute readers this does not come as a particularly surprising revelation, but we can use it to further examine our tendencies!

You see, our brain really likes to avoid feeling bad; it's the cause of all our favourite emotions like fear, hunger, and anxiety to name a few. It's one of the most effective ways your brain gets you to take actions it thinks are necessary. But sadly our brain sometimes does not understand all the following consequences of avoiding the things that hurt.

For example, if you need to study and you are procrastinating, what are the possible bad emotions? Well maybe the work is boring, maybe you are behind and it doesn't feel nice to think about how behind you are and the consequences of doing badly, so you lie to yourself and tell yourself you'll study soon but right now I need to write this article! This is a deal your brain will happily take: "Are you telling me that I just need to finish this article and then I'll do all the studying in the world to make things right? Hell yeah!" NO! You are just a stupid brain, making stupid decisions. Sometimes it's a bit more obvious than that, if you planned a gym session or a run, you may not want to do it, because well, it just straight up hurts physically.

But it's okay, I have some advice for you. I've found some things that have helped me, and hopefully they can help you too!

The Count of Monte Cristo

My favourite book I've read so far; I really loved it! It's the reason I first found an interest in sailing and ships.

The book is quite long, around 300k words, meaning you get plenty of time to really get absorbed into the story. One characteristic of old books is that they will just go into such unexpected detail, and write a novelette on the back story of one side character which isn't even pivotal to the story. I guess this is why people read abridged versions, but I think you'd be missing out! Just embrace the change of pace and settle into the story.

Another characteristic of the book is that it has a narrator speaking to you. I feel like this isn't really a thing anymore with books and it's very interesting, I'll definitely do that when or if I get around to writing a novel of my own.

I read the public domain translation of the book, and while it was sufficient, I did find it quite hard to follow at times, there are some more recent and more easily readable translations available and I definitely recommend those. It won't completely change the vibe of it all, but it will make it easier to understand the story.

Sometimes when I am thinking about the theme of struggle and perseverance I will think of this book, and draw inspiration from Dantès, which is curious considering it is a fictional book after all. But do not fall into the trap that fictional literature cannot be good for the mind and soul. Fictional media is describing the human experience after all. Written by humans, for humans, centering around human themes and stories. I think a healthy media diet definitely includes fictional works as well as other kinds if you want to be a well rounded human.
