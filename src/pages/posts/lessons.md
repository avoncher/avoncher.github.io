## Don't look down on other people

Growing up, I would often look down on other people who I thought I was better than. I developed various identities throughout my childhood for various reasons, and as a result I would look down on people without realising why I was doing that! And also without realising the impacts of doing so.

You also don't realise that you don't see the full picture. Often it comes from quite a privileged position and lack of hardship. It's easy to assume that other people are lazy, or uncommited, or unmotivated, or stupid or whatever adjective you choose to use when you haven't lived life in their shoes. This is probably the biggest lesson I've learnt and it took me moving out and living without my parents to learn it.

When you look down on other people you make your own life harder, because the way that you judge other people, you assume they judge you.

## Take shit seriously!

When nothing matters, everything becomes boring. When you start to give a shit about things, you start to become invested in the outcome, and the process, and you feel like you're working towards something. The effort you put towards something no longer feels meaningless.

I think you should apply this to as many things as you can. Take your friendships seriously, take your hobbies seriously, when you're cleaning your room, take it seriously! I think it's really a shame how the idea of "minimum wage = minimum effort" became so prevalent amongst gen-z; you start to really dread your work, you don't take pride in it, it just feels boring! I'm not saying you should be taken advantage of, but I do think that putting in serious effort is _not_ you being taken advantage of. Chances are your work conditions actually become better, despite how you may feel about whether or not hard work is rewarded in these sorts of jobs.

I'm also not even saying that you shouldn't relax. Take your relaxation seriously too! Don't watch that movie whilst scrolling on your phone, put that shit away and focus!

I've also noticed that current me is often grateful that past me did various things in the past that were optional but now is giving current me more opportunities and choice as I figure out what to do in life. There's also been plenty of annoyances about the things I didn't do because I wasn't taking things seriously.

## Use a greedy algorithm when you don't know what to do

Sometimes I find myself really struggling, maybe theres a lot that I need to do in life, and theres a million stressors. One thing that really calms me down is a strong belief in the following: when you don't know what your plan of action should be, just take the next best choice. Sometimes it's not even best choice once you consider all the paths, but picking a good choice is far better than being paralysed into not making any choices and falling further behind.

I think this applies less for long term plans, and more so the short term plans, i.e. less than a year long. For example catching up in uni, needing to find a job, starting to get stressed about money, having issues with friends or family, etc. But as it all starts to pile up it can feel very overwhelming.

You don't need to plan out your life, you just need to go to sleep. You don't need to make a whole ass plan for a thing, just start by watching a lecture that you need to, doesn't matter which. Just start with a thing you need to do and stop procrastinating by worrying about the best way to do it! If you imagine a version of your life forked from current you, and that person just makes the best choice at each step, its hard to imagine them not being in a place of life that is appealing.

## Don't optimise for easiness

So your brain loves feeling nice. Of course it does, its a stupid brain living in the stone age (or whatever came before that)! In the past we had to work pretty hard to feel nice. Food was a lot harder to come by, particularly food with hard to come by nutrients hence why some foods are just delicious! We also had to save a lot of energy, just because the equation of inputs and expenditure was harder to balance.

The problem is, we need to fight this natural urge of optimising for easiness and the effort/reward ratio, due to living in a modern world. Life no longer has this huge factor of effort/reward. Food is quite easy to come by now, and tasty, calorically dense food is especially easy. Hits of dopamine is easier than ever, with entertainment of all sorts being ubiquitous. Even 300 years ago, the richest nobles could not have virtually any cuisine at their doorstep the way that we can now with food delivery apps.

If we just did what was easiest, we would play video games all day and watch youtube videos and escape to whatever world as soon as real life starts getting uncomfortable. Discomforts exist for a reason! They're there to motivate you to change your situation. Hating your job, or the feeling of hunger, all these feelings exist for a reason!

So fight this urge, be aware of it, and try to embrace delayed gratification.

## Keep plans private and don't day dream!

Theres this phrase, and I don't remember how it goes so give me a second. hmmmm. There are those that do and those who uhhhhhhhh. There are those who do and those who say they'll do? That can't be it. Basically, there are sayers and doers. When you get into a bad habit of saying you'll do all sorts of things, it can be hard to follow through on all of them and then people start to not take you at your word, and this is a pretty bad feeling. It can even feel like they don't believe in you, and I mean, they sorta don't. If you're in the sayer camp, chances are they're probably right; I mean human change is very hard after all.

But theres also another problem. People can take you seriously! Especially if you've been in a rut of sorts, it can be very exciting for the people around you to hear that you acknowledge it and that you've got a plan to fix it. It makes you feel so good to be supported, and you get so excited about the future and you start to day dream. The problem is that it can feel so good telling people about your plans (i.e. saying!) that you realise you don't need to go through with them in order to get the good feeling (i.e. lack of doing!)! So the fix to this is simple. Instead of telling people you have all these ideas and plans, just tell them when you've done it, they'll be just as excited and theres a much higher chance that you follow through, and a much lower chance that you feel shame after not following through with your plans you told everyone about for the millionth time. Also this shame feeling can make it really hard to make changes in your life.
